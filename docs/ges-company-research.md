## 2. What GES does

GES is a **general services contractor (GSC)** and experiential marketing company for live events. It sells to two main kinds of customer:

1. **Event organizers** (associations, trade show owners, corporations): full show management. That covers floor plans, booth/stand build, carpet, signage and graphics, furniture, material handling (drayage), freight and logistics, electrical, rigging, labor, and registration.
2. **Exhibitors and brands**: rental and custom exhibits, turnkey "Show Ready" stands (EMEA), and brand activations through Spiro.

GES earns money from organizers (show management contracts) and also directly from exhibitors (orders for exhibitor services such as material handling, electrical and furniture). The organizer contract is the "gateway" that unlocks exhibitor revenue at each show.

### The "GES Collective": brands and business units

## 4. Ownership history and the Truelink deal

- **Viad era:** GES was Viad's largest segment for decades, next to Pursuit (attractions). Viad reported GES in two segments: **GES Exhibitions** and **Spiro**.
- **2022:** Viad sold **ON Services**, a non-core audio-visual business (~$50M revenue headwind in 2023).
- **Oct 21, 2024:** Viad agreed to sell GES to Truelink for **$535M** cash. Moelis and Latham & Watkins advised Viad. Baird and BrightTower (financial) and Kirkland & Ellis (legal) advised Truelink. Stax did sell-side commercial diligence.
- **Dec 31, 2024:** The deal closed and GES became a standalone, PE-backed company. Viad renamed itself **Pursuit (PRSU)**.

**Implied valuation:** about **0.6x FY2023 revenue** and **about 7.8x FY2023 Adj. EBITDA** ($535M / $68.2M; my arithmetic, ignoring cash/debt adjustments). [Axia Growth](https://axiagrowth.com/resources/industries/business-services/event-production-services) independently puts it at about 0.60x revenue.

### About Truelink Capital
- Middle-market PE firm in Los Angeles. Focuses on **industrials and tech-enabled / business services**, often **corporate carve-outs**. GES was its sixth acquisition.
- Fund I was **$950M**. Truelink now reports **over $4.3B AUM** (Nov 2026).
- Its stated playbook is to "accelerate growth through the execution of strategic initiatives and **transformative add-on acquisitions**."
- Other recent deals include JT Thorpe Group and Johnson Controls' Global Intrusion Products business, so it is an active carve-out buyer.

> At close, Luke Myers said Truelink would "invest in its core strengths in creative design, logistics and event management, **pursue growth through strategic acquisitions, and expand its agency presence**."
>
> At the CSG deal (Nov 2026): "we continue to see a **robust pipeline of M&A opportunities** to build on the platform."

This is a classic **PE buy-and-build** platform: a large carve-out bought at a modest multiple, with smaller add-ons bought at lower multiples to raise blended margin and widen services.

Sources: [Truelink close](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/truelink-capital-and-viad-close-transaction-to-establish-ges-as-independent-business-302340697.html), [Private Equity International](https://www.privateequityinternational.com/truelink-capital-completes-535m-carve-out-buyout-of-event-management-firm-ges/), [Baird deal card](https://www.rwbaird.com/transactions/investment-banking/dealcard/6427/), [Stax](https://www.stax.com/news/stax-provides-sell-side-support-to-ges-a-global-exhibition-and-event-leader-on-its-acquisition-by-truelink-capital)

## 5. Financials (last public data, under Viad)

GES has been private since 2025 and no longer publishes financials. These are the last reported numbers.

| ($M) | FY2021 | FY2022 | FY2023 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GES Exhibitions revenue** | 209.5 | 557.9 | — |
| **Spiro revenue** | 116.6 | 277.6 | — |
| **Total GES revenue** | 320.3 | 828.0 | **888.4** |
| **Total GES Adj. EBITDA** | (30.4) | 61.3 | **68.2** |
| **Adj. EBITDA margin** | -9.5% | 7.4% | ~7.7% |

- 2021 is the COVID trough. 2022 shows the rebound.
- In 2022, Spiro had the higher margin (9.7% vs 6.1% for Exhibitions). That helps explain why Truelink is building out the **agency** side (Spiro, 2Heads, CSG).
- **Show rotation:** many large shows run every 2–4 years (e.g., MINExpo, CONEXPO-type events), so revenue is lumpy from year to year.
- **Labor:** about **100 collective bargaining agreements**, with about a third renegotiated each year (Viad 10-K). Union labor is a major integration and diligence factor.

## 6. Acquisition history: what GES has actually bought

| Date | Target | Type | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oct 2014 | **onPeak** + **TPHousing** | Event housing | Adjacent service sold to the same organizers |
| Mar 2017 | **Poken** (Palo Alto) | NFC event-networking tech | Event tech / attendee data |
| 2018 (Viad) | ON Services | AV (later **divested 2022**) | AV was not core; sold off |
| **Dec 2, 2025** | **2Heads** (London; LA, Montreal) | Experiential / creative agency | 1st Truelink add-on: grows Spiro in UK/EU, adds aerospace, tech and FS clients. PwC advised the seller. |
| **Nov 5, 2026** | **CSG Creative** (Alexandria, VA), from National Trade Productions | Association marketing agency | 2nd Truelink add-on: attendee and membership marketing. Keeps its own brand and leadership. Two-way cross-sell. |

Organic expansion that points to where they may buy next:
- **Paris office and warehouse** (GES EMEA into France)
- **Qatar division** (Middle East)
- New Quebec/Ottawa and Toronto GMs (Canada)
- Show Ready – The Edit (premium sustainable stands), event-emissions reporting (ESG)

### The pattern so far (my analysis)
1. **Agency and creative first.** Both Truelink deals are higher-margin marketing and creative shops, not more trade show contractors.
2. **Moving "up the funnel."** CSG lets GES sell the organizer *attendee marketing* as well as *show execution*: "a single partner from attendee marketing through show execution."
3. **Brand kept, team kept.** Targets keep their name and leadership as "operating companies within GES." That points to a **holding-company / collective** model, not full absorption.
4. **Geographic fill-ins.** 2Heads brought London, LA and Montreal. Organic moves into France and Qatar show where GES wants more presence.
5. **Undisclosed prices.** Both add-ons are small (price not disclosed), typical of PE tuck-ins.

## 7. Competitive landscape

GES is one of the two largest North American trade show GSCs. The market is consolidating, and PE money is flowing in.

| Company | Ownership | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Freeman** | Family-owned. KKR is a strategic (minority/financing) partner. | #1 in the market. Bought brand agency **Sparks** (2023) with KKR financing. GES's main rival. |
| **Shepard** | Employee-owned (Atlanta) | Founded 1905; nationwide GSC + AV + exhibits |
| **Fern Exposition & Event Services** | Private (Cincinnati) | 501–1,000 employees; full-service GSC |
| **Heritage Exposition Services** | Employee-owned | Nationwide GSC |
| **The Expo Group** | **New State Capital** (majority, 2024) | Irving, TX; ~100 shows a year |
| **Nth Degree** | **Shamrock Capital** (majority, 2024) | Labor and event management, ~4,000 events a year |
| **Expo Convention Contractors** | Private (founded 1995) | Regional GSC |
| **Skyline Exhibits** | **Gemspring Capital** (2020) | Exhibit design / dealer network, doing dealer roll-ups |
| **Encore** (ex-PSAV) | **Blackstone**. IPO filing Apr 2026. | AV giant: $3.4B revenue, ~2,200 exclusive venue contracts. Bought agency FIRST (~$97.5M). |

**Other recent sector deals:** Apollo took **Emerald** private (closed Jul 14, 2026; ~$1.5B EV, ~11.8x EBITDA) and agreed to buy **Questex**. Clarion Capital bought **Marketplace Events** (Jan 2025). KKR and Ares took control of **PRG** (Oct 2025). Clair Global made four acquisitions in 2025. One Rock bought **American Furniture Rentals** (event furniture). An industry tracker counts **2,186 event-services transactions** from Q3 2021 to Q2 2026.

**Market backdrop:** trade show volume is *still below 2019*. The CEIR Total Index was 11.1% below 2019 in Q3 2025 and is forecast to grow +2.1% in 2026. Corporate event spend is growing faster, at 8–10% CAGR since 2019 per Encore. **Growth through acquisition matters more when the core market grows slowly**, which supports the buy-and-build thesis.

## 8. Likely acquisition targets (my analysis)

Based on what Truelink and GES have said and bought, these target types look most likely. This section is inference, not reported fact.

| Category | Why it fits | Example profile |
|---|---|---|
| **Regional / independent GSCs** | Win show contracts and venue exclusives in cities where GES is thin. Consolidate competitors. | Family-owned decorators with $10–100M revenue and long organizer contracts |
| **Association marketing agencies** | Repeat the CSG playbook: retainer revenue, cross-sell into GES organizer relationships | DC/Virginia/Chicago association agencies |
| **Experiential / creative agencies** | Grow Spiro's margin and corporate (non-trade-show) exposure | Like 2Heads: 20–200 people, blue-chip brand clients |
| **Event technology** | Registration, lead retrieval, badging, attendee data. Extends Visit and onPeak. | SaaS with recurring revenue in event registration |
| **Specialty services** | Power & lighting, rigging, AV, freight/logistics, furniture rental | Companies like SHOWTECH; furniture/rental fleets |
| **Geographic expansion** | EMEA (France, DACH, Middle East), India, Canada | Local stand-builders, Middle East contractors |

**What buyers in this sector check most** (per Axia): venue contracts and exclusivity, repeat clients and show recurrence (normalized for rotation), equipment age and utilization, how variable the cost structure is, union / CBA exposure, and wage-and-hour compliance for freelance crews. Deals often include **deferred or earn-out consideration** tied to keeping clients.

## 9. What this means for the M&A application

These are notes for later, as context only. Nothing has been built.

GES runs a **repeatable buy-and-build program** across many target types, keeps acquired brands as operating companies, and answers to a PE sponsor that tracks value creation. A useful tool would likely need:

- **Target pipeline / CRM:** a universe of GSCs, agencies, event-tech and specialty firms, with stages (sourced → contacted → NDA → IOI → LOI → diligence → closed) and owner/sponsor details. Many targets are family- or employee-owned, which changes how you approach them.
- **Fit scoring** built around GES's thesis: service-line adjacency, geography gaps, overlap with GES organizer clients (cross-sell), recurring/retainer share, and venue exclusives.
- **Show and contract mapping:** which shows and venues a target holds, contract end dates, and rotation cycle (2–4-year shows).
- **Valuation helpers:** revenue/EBITDA multiples using sector comps (GES ~0.6x rev / ~7.8x EBITDA; Emerald ~11.8x; agency deals), plus deferred and earn-out structures.
- **Diligence checklists** specific to events: CBAs and union jurisdictions, freelance labor classification, equipment inventory, customer concentration, show-rotation-normalized revenue.
- **Integration tracking** for a "collective" model: brand kept, leadership retention, cross-sell wins tracked, synergy capture.
- **Sponsor reporting:** pipeline and portfolio dashboards for Truelink.
